Sorensen et al. (2024) distinguish three forms
of pluralism. Overton pluralism concerns the
range of positions a model represents. Steerable pluralism concerns its ability to adopt a requested position. Distributional pluralism concerns whether output frequencies reflect a target
population. Our benchmark measures Overtonstyle coverage: whether documented perspectives
are recovered at all.
The concentration of training, preference, and
evaluation data in WEIRD and English-language
settings raises concerns about uneven cultural representation (Sorensen et al., 2024; Wang et al.,
2025; Zewail et al., 2026). Multilingual and culturally adaptive prompting can improve diversity
in some settings (Wang et al., 2025). Languagespecific evaluation resources also remain unevenly
developed. Serbian SuperGLUE addresses gaps
in complex language-understanding evaluation for
Serbian and motivates broader coverage of South

misses roughly three of every ten reference perspectives on average.

Measuring perspective coverage
Political controversies rarely have a complete, uncontested set of correct labels. Perspectivist NLP
therefore treats disagreement as part of the phenomenon being studied (Basile et al., 2021; Plank,
2022; Frenda et al., 2025). Related approaches retain annotator-specific rationales or label distributions rather than combining all judgments into one
target (Sarumi et al., 2026; Shetty et al., 2026). A
fixed reference set can document positions known
to exist, but cannot guarantee an exhaustive account of a debate.

opinion. Under some prompting strategies, models can reproduce population-level polling patterns
(Sun et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2026). Their apparent political or cultural preferences can also
change with prompt design and methodology
