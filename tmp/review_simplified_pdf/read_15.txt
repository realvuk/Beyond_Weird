C.5
Missing judgments and the recall cap
With a complete and identical judge panel across
the five generations, the summed panel credit for
a reference cannot exceed one. Missing judge
calls can change the denominator from one generation to another, however. In that case, summing generation-level weights can produce a value
slightly above one.
The implementation therefore caps each reference credit at one before topic recall is computed.
The cap affects two observed credits for Grok 4.3
on the WEIRD topic in pair 8: their uncapped values are 1.0167 and 1.0917. Both are set to 1 in the
published scores.

The design requires 1,040 × 5 = 5,200 judge
decisions. The raw judgment log contains 5, 228
records because failed calls that were retried remain in the log. Of these records, 44 are failed or

unrecovered.

valid decisions. Of the 1, 040 generated perspectives, 1, 024 have five valid judge decisions and 16
have four. A no-match decision is a valid judgment in which the judge selects no reference. A
missing judgment is an execution failure. The two

Supplementary interval and upper bound.
The original percentile bootstrap resamples the
eight matched pairs 10,000 times, keeping all 13
models together within each sampled pair. It uses
seed 42 and the saved random-number stream, giving [−0.08096, 0.05823]. With only eight resampling units, percentile intervals may have limited
coverage accuracy. The corresponding one-sided
95% t upper bound is 0.059. This is conditional on
the interval assumptions and the selected benchmark, not a general upper bound on cultural disparities.
