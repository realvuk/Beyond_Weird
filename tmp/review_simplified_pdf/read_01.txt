Matched WEIRD and Serbian Debates Show No Detectable WEIRD
Advantage in LLM Perspective Coverage
Anonymous TACL submission
Abstract
Coverage of different perspectives may depend on numerous factors. How frequently positions appear in training data, how readily models retrieve them, and whether post-training or
safety procedures discourage their expression.
These pressures may affect populations unevenly.
The concentration of language-model development in Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich,
and Democratic (WEIRD) (Henrich et al., 2010)
and English-language settings motivates concern
about cultural representation (Sorensen et al.,
2024; Feng et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025; Zewail
et al., 2026). We therefore hypothesise that models
recover perspectives more completely on WEIRD
controversies than on Serbian controversies.
Testing this expectation requires attention to
topic selection. Political controversies differ in
documentation, history, sensitivity, and structure.
A comparison of unrelated WEIRD and Serbian
topics could attribute differences in topic difficulty
to cultural setting. We reduce this ambiguity by
comparing structurally analogous controversies.
The benchmark contains 16 topics arranged
into eight WEIRD-Serbian pairs. Each topic has
five reference perspectives documented in surveys, polling, referendum results, peer-reviewed
research, or clearly attested public debate. These
references provide a fixed evaluation target but
they do not exhaust the legitimate positions on an
issue. Thirteen LLMs each generate five perspectives per topic in English. Five LLM judges independently match each generated perspective to
one of the references or return no match. No judge
evaluates a generator from its own model family.
Recall then measures how much of the reference
set the outputs recover.
We address three research questions:
Large language models can describe political controversies accurately while omitting relevant perspectives. We test whether
they recover fewer documented perspectives on Serbian controversies than on structurally matched topics from Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich, and Democratic
(WEIRD) settings. The benchmark pairs
eight Serbian controversies with WEIRD
counterparts. Each of the 16 topics has five
reference perspectives documented in surveys, polling, referendum results, research,
or public debate. Thirteen LLMs each generated five perspectives per topic in English,
and five LLM judges from other developers matched each output to a reference or
returned no match. Mean recall was 0.581
on WEIRD topics and 0.594 on Serbian topics. The mean WEIRD-minus-Serbian difference across the eight pairs was −1.3 percentage points (95% CI: −10.3 to 7.7), with
no evidence for the hypothesised WEIRD
advantage (p = 0.680). Differences between individual pairs (−16.9 to +19.1
points) were much larger than the pooled
difference. Inter-judge agreement was similar across the two settings, although agreement does not establish accuracy. Coverage
was incomplete in both settings, and the result does not establish equivalent coverage.
Introduction
An LLM answering a controversial political question selects both facts and perspectives. An answer about Kosovo’s independence, immigration,
or historical responsibility may be factually accurate yet omit positions held by people involved in
the debate. As language models mediate access to
political information, we need to evaluate which
perspectives their answers recover.
