Coverage of different perspectives may depend on numerous factors. How frequently positions appear in training data, how readily models retrieve them, and whether post-training or
safety procedures discourage their expression.
These pressures may affect populations unevenly.
The concentration of language-model development in Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich,
and Democratic (WEIRD) (Henrich et al., 2010)
and English-language settings motivates concern
about cultural representation (Sorensen et al.,
2024; Feng et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025; Zewail
et al., 2026). We therefore hypothesise that models
recover perspectives more completely on WEIRD
controversies than on Serbian controversies.
Testing this expectation requires attention to
topic selection. Political controversies differ in
documentation, history, sensitivity, and structure.
A comparison of unrelated WEIRD and Serbian
topics could attribute differences in topic difficulty
to cultural setting. We reduce this ambiguity by
comparing structurally analogous controversies.
The benchmark contains 16 topics arranged
into eight WEIRD-Serbian pairs. Each topic has
five reference perspectives documented in surveys, polling, referendum results, peer-reviewed
research, or clearly attested public debate. These
references provide a fixed evaluation target but
they do not exhaust the legitimate positions on an
issue. Thirteen LLMs each generate five perspectives per topic in English. Five LLM judges independently match each generated perspective to
one of the references or return no match. No judge
evaluates a generator from its own model family.
Recall then measures how much of the reference
set the outputs recover.
We address three research questions:

Large language models can describe political controversies accurately while omitting relevant perspectives. We test whether
they recover fewer documented perspectives on Serbian controversies than on structurally matched topics from Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich, and Democratic
(WEIRD) settings. The benchmark pairs
eight Serbian controversies with WEIRD
counterparts. Each of the 16 topics has five
reference perspectives documented in surveys, polling, referendum results, research,
or public debate. Thirteen LLMs each generated five perspectives per topic in English,
and five LLM judges from other developers matched each output to a reference or
returned no match. Mean recall was 0.581
on WEIRD topics and 0.594 on Serbian topics. The mean WEIRD-minus-Serbian difference across the eight pairs was −1.3 percentage points (95% CI: −10.3 to 7.7), with
no evidence for the hypothesised WEIRD
advantage (p = 0.680). Differences between individual pairs (−16.9 to +19.1
points) were much larger than the pooled
difference. Inter-judge agreement was similar across the two settings, although agreement does not establish accuracy. Coverage
was incomplete in both settings, and the result does not establish equivalent coverage.

Measuring perspective coverage

Political controversies rarely have a complete, uncontested set of correct labels. Perspectivist NLP
therefore treats disagreement as part of the phenomenon being studied (Basile et al., 2021; Plank,
2022; Frenda et al., 2025). Related approaches retain annotator-specific rationales or label distributions rather than combining all judgments into one
target (Sarumi et al., 2026; Shetty et al., 2026). A
fixed reference set can document positions known
to exist, but cannot guarantee an exhaustive account of a debate.
Other work uses LLMs as proxies for human
opinion. Under some prompting strategies, models can reproduce population-level polling patterns
(Sun et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2026). Their apparent political or cultural preferences can also
change with prompt design and methodology
(Khan et al., 2025). Hybrid approaches combine

2.3

Evaluating generated perspectives

Evaluating coverage requires deciding whether
differently worded statements express the same
stance. LLM judges can assess this relationship,
but their agreement with human assessments can
vary across the models being evaluated (PooleDayan et al., 2025).
We use five judges, present each generated
perspective separately, randomise reference order,
and exclude judges from the generator’s model
family. We also report inter-judge agreement and
compare it across WEIRD and Serbian topics.
These checks assess consistency of the matching
procedure. They cannot establish correctness, because several judges may share an error, and they
do not replace human validation.

A reference perspective is a substantively distinct
stance documented as being held within the target population. The first author constructed all
80 reference perspectives manually from research
literature and news reporting, drawing on surveys, polling, referendum results, peer-reviewed
research, and clearly attested public debate. The
reference statements summarise positions supported by these sources, rather than positions generated by the models evaluated here. The first author selected five distinct stances per topic from
the linked sources. The source workbook records
a source entry for each of the 80 perspectives. The
accompanying source ledger links the evaluated
reference identifiers to their workbook cells and
preserves both the source text and any embedded
hyperlink. All reference summaries and explanations in the workbook match the evaluated reference set exactly. Source entries identify the evidence used in construction.
Reference summaries and explanations were
written in English. Sources cited for Serbian topics include English-language research, reporting,
and survey materials. The language of the cited
material was therefore not uniformly Serbian.
Each topic has five references so that all topics
share the same evaluation budget. This is a design choice, not a claim that a controversy has ex-

5.1

Interpreting the cultural comparison

Research on pluralistic and culturally adaptive
alignment motivated our expectation of better coverage in WEIRD contexts (Sorensen et al., 2024;
Feng et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025). This benchmark does not support it for the selected controversies.
The primary matched-pair comparison provides
no evidence for the predicted WEIRD recall advantage. Judge agreement was also similar across
the two settings, although this does not establish
equal error rates. These findings concern the selected controversies under direct elicitation in English. They do not establish equal representation
across cultures, languages, or tasks.
Several explanations remain possible. Contemporary training data may contain enough regional and multilingual material to recover welldocumented Serbian positions when models are
explicitly asked for diverse perspectives. Retrieval
limitations or post-training behaviour may constrain coverage across controversies more generally. The five-perspective output limit also creates
competition among legitimate positions for inclusion. The experiment does not distinguish these
explanations.
Topic selection matters when interpreting the
result. Variation across controversies was much
larger than the pooled cultural difference, with
large pair differences favouring both settings. An

5.2

Incomplete coverage remains

The absence of a detectable WEIRD advantage
does not imply comprehensive coverage. Even the
strongest models omit a substantial share of the
reference perspectives. In this benchmark, omissions occur across both cultural settings rather
than consistently concentrating in one.
Recall varies within each access and size category, and individual scores overlap across categories. Although the observed category means
differ, none of the four exploratory comparisons
reaches the adjusted significance threshold. These
results do not establish equivalence between categories or identify the effects of size and openness
separately.
Future work could examine what makes particular controversies or perspectives difficult to recover. Candidate factors include the amount and
visibility of documentation, the prominence of positions in training data, and their responsiveness
to elicitation after post-training. Measuring these
properties directly would allow them to be tested
as predictors, rather than inferred from cultural or
model-level averages.
5.3

Implications for evaluation and culturally
adaptive systems

The matched design can support comparisons involving other populations. Its purpose is to make
differences in perspective coverage easier to interpret by accounting for topic selection. Coverage
should also be evaluated directly. General capability, multilingual performance, and overall answer
quality do not establish that a model will recover
the positions relevant to a contested issue.
The appropriate intervention depends on the observed deficit. A systematic cultural coverage gap
may motivate targeted cultural or linguistic adaptation. If coverage is incomplete across both sides
of a matched comparison, improving the retrieval
or elicitation of omitted perspectives may be more
relevant. The present result does not make cultural adaptation unnecessary, instead it helps specify what an intervention would need to address.
This distinction also matters for AI-mediated
deliberation. Systems that expose disagreement,

support consensus, or introduce minority positions depend on the perspectives available to them
(Tessler et al., 2024; Chiang et al., 2024; Lee et al.,
2025). A perspective that never enters the candidate set cannot inform those later processes. Perspective recall is thus a prerequisite for their use of
diverse positions, not a substitute for deliberation
itself.

The reference sets are not exhaustive

Each topic has five externally documented reference perspectives that a single author constructed.
Reference statements for Serbian topics are English summaries of sources in more than one language, so summarisation and translation choices
may affect how closely model outputs match them.
This common target supports comparison across
topics, but it cannot represent every legitimate position within a population. Recall therefore measures coverage of the reference set. Similarly, an
output that matches no reference receives no precision credit even if it expresses a legitimate position
outside the benchmark. Absolute recall and precision values should be read in this context. Their
main value here is comparison under a common
construction procedure.
6.2

Judge agreement does not establish judge
validity

The scores depend on LLM judgments of whether
generated and reference perspectives express the
same stance.
Agreement and judge-removal
checks describe consistency and sensitivity to
panel membership. They do not establish correctness, because judges may share errors. Excluding
judges from the generator’s developer family prevents direct within-family evaluation but does not
remove shared biases or differences in judge strictness.

The paper tests direct elicitation at one

We explicitly asked models for diverse perspectives. The benchmark therefore measures recovery

eliminate topic confounding

The pairs share broad political structures, but may
differ in documentation, historical depth, polarisation, and training-data prevalence. Matching improves the interpretability of the comparison without experimentally isolating cultural setting. It
was investigator-guided rather than based on a prespecified quantitative rule, and alternative pairings
may be defensible.
Releasing the topic definitions, matching rationales, reference perspectives, and their supporting
sources will allow others to inspect these choices
and test alternative pairings.

7

Conclusion

Across eight matched WEIRD-Serbian topic pairs
and 13 generators, we did not detect the predicted
WEIRD recall advantage. Differences between individual pairs were much larger than the pooled
difference, and coverage remained incomplete in
both settings and varied across models and controversies. The main methodological implication
is to measure perspective coverage directly, using
comparisons that account for topic selection and
examining which perspectives are omitted on particular controversies.
